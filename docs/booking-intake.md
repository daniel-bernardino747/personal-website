# Booking intake form

The questions attached to the cal.com event `get-off-the-ground`, and how the answers turn into a 15-minute conversation.

Written in English because the booking page sits behind an English site and an English CTA ("Get it off the ground"). The Portuguese version lives at [`booking-intake.pt.md`](booking-intake.pt.md) — pick one funnel language and keep only that file.

## The rule

Every question must either **disqualify the call before it happens** or **remove a minute from it**. A question that only satisfies curiosity is a question that costs bookings — each added field drops completion. Five fields is the ceiling.

## The form

Cal.com field types in brackets. Name and email are the built-ins; these come after.

### 1. What exists today? — required *[Radio Group]*

- Nothing yet — it's an idea
- A document, spec, or spreadsheet
- A prototype or MVP that runs
- A product in production with real users

*Why:* sets the entire shape of the call before it starts. "Nothing yet" and "in production" are two different conversations, and knowing which one saves the first five minutes of discovery.

### 2. What's blocking you right now? — required *[Long Text]*

Placeholder: `Two or three sentences. The thing that's actually stuck, not the whole backstory.`

*Why:* this is the only question that can't be inferred. It's also the honest filter — someone who can't name a block in three sentences doesn't have one yet.

### 3. If this works, what's true in 90 days? — required *[Short Text]*

Placeholder: `First paying user. Internal team off the spreadsheet. Demo that survives investors.`

*Why:* turns a vague ask into a target. Without it the call ends in agreement and no decision.

### 4. Link to anything that exists — optional *[URL]*

Placeholder: `Repo, Figma, deck, landing page, even the spreadsheet.`

*Why:* five minutes reading this beforehand is worth fifteen minutes of description on the call.

### 5. Who else has to say yes? — optional *[Short Text]*

*Why:* names the real decision-maker early. If it isn't the person booking, the call's goal changes from "decide" to "arm them to convince someone else."

## The 15 minutes

| Minutes | What happens | Fed by |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Play back the block in my words, get it corrected | Q2 |
| 2–6 | What actually exists, what's load-bearing, what's disposable | Q1, Q4 |
| 6–11 | Shortest path to the 90-day outcome — named, not brainstormed | Q3 |
| 11–14 | The first step, and whether it needs me at all | Q1, Q5 |
| 14–15 | One next action with a date, or an honest no | — |

Prep is reading the four answers and the link. If prep takes longer than five minutes, the form is asking for too little or the call is scoped too big.

## Ending early is a feature

Say so in the first five minutes when:

- The block in Q2 is a **hiring** problem or a **sales** problem wearing a technical mask
- Q1 is "nothing yet" **and** Q3 is unanswerable in one sentence — that's a positioning conversation, not a build one
- The real answer is a tool they can buy today

Ending at minute six with a straight "this isn't me, here's who it is" is what makes the promise on the booking page true. The description already commits to it — *"if it's not me, I'll tell you that too."*
